# APEX RACING MANAGER
### Official Field Manual — v0.5 "The Owner's Edition"

*You own the team. You sign the cheques. And on Sunday, you climb into the car yourself.*

---

## 1. THE PREMISE

Apex Racing Manager is a full motorsport management sim wrapped around Automobilista 2. Everything between race weekends — money, people, politics, development — happens in the manager. Then you launch AMS2 and drive the race with your own hands. Your real result flows back and the world reacts. No simulated Sundays. Your hands, your championship.

## 2. FOUNDING YOUR TEAM

Career creation decides who you are: name and colors, your **origin story** (eight of them — Former Racing Driver to Lottery Winner — setting opening capital, reputation, and crucially *who sits on your board*), your **launch posture** (bare-bones to aggressive — scales starting capital), and your **philosophy** (decision tempo, recruitment focus, risk appetite). Every choice has real mechanical teeth, shown honestly before you sign the founding papers.

## 3. THE BOARD & OWNERSHIP

The people who funded your launch expect results. Each entered series carries a **mandate**; the board pays season budgets scaled by their **funding generosity**, judges you with **patience and strikes**, and settles a **season bonus & penalty** at year's end. But the board is not forever: you start owning 40–60% of the team, and you can **buy them out in tranches** (a happy board sells dear, a frustrated one sells cheap) or sell equity when desperate. Cross 50% and mandates become advisory. Reach 100% and you answer to no one — and no one writes you cheques. **Team valuation** tracks everything you build; outside buyers occasionally come knocking.

## 4. MONEY

Cash burns weekly: wages, operations, facility upkeep, finance installments. Income arrives from sponsors (retainers, race fees, bonuses), prize money, board grants, the **Road to Round 1** pre-season milestone checklist, **client track days** (a genuine business — pick the client, the car, and your asking rate), and **exhibition invitationals**. Cars can be bought outright, **financed** (30% down, ten weeks, 6%, repossession after three missed payments), or **leased** (weekly fee, minimum term, never yours). Run dry and the **solvency spiral** begins: warning → distress (suppliers tighten) → administration (spending locked, a last-chance investor, a four-week countdown) → collapse. Every stage has escape levers. Use them.

## 5. THE CAR

Each car wears per system — engine, gearbox, brakes, suspension, chassis — through races, test days, and client work. Service it in the workshop, pass **scrutineering** before rounds, and watch condition: a worn car makes the opposition genuinely faster on Sunday (see §12).

## 6. R&D

Ten projects across **Aero, Powertrain, Reliability, Operations** — sequential tiers, real costs, weeks of work. Your engineering department's strength drives speed and quality; a test day in a project's final stretch **validates** it (faster, safer sign-off). Aero and Powertrain sharpen your package against the field; Reliability cuts wear and DNF risk; Operations cuts service bills. Spec series block performance R&D — the rules are the rules. A level-3 Workshop unlocks a second concurrent project.

## 7. HQ FACILITIES

Five buildings, three levels each, weekly upkeep: **Workshop** (service speed/cost, the second R&D slot), **Design Studio** (R&D quality), **Simulator** (test-day yield, delegated performance), **Hospitality Suite** (sponsor warmth, client income), **Logistics Bay** (freight costs, away-round reliability). One construction at a time.

## 8. STAFF

Engineers, mechanics, commercial, operations — real people with ratings, wages, morale, and contracts. Department strength quietly touches everything: sponsor negotiations, car wear, test days, service quotes, delegated weekends. Scout, hire, renegotiate, and watch morale — unhappy people get poached.

## 9. DRIVERS & THE SECOND CAR

Every driver in the world — 800+ — holds a real contract. **Approach anyone**: free agents negotiate wages; contracted drivers cost a buyout, and their team can refuse (and will remember). Scout for hidden gems. Field a **second car**: buy a real car, hire a driver (including **pay drivers** who bring backing money but not pace), and they race *in the actual AMS2 grid* under their own name on that car's livery — their genuine result scores your points. Drivers age, develop, and decline; **silly season** reshuffles the grids every winter; strugglers get sacked mid-season.

## 10. SPONSORS

Five slot tiers from Supplier to **Title partner**. Small deals run per-race; big ones run full seasons or multi-year with option clauses and break fees. **Pitch with promises** — attendance, livery prominence, podiums, PR days — and they become enforceable contract clauses, settled at season's end. **Vet brands before signing** (slow payers, aggressive lawyers, collapse risks — an unvetted collapse claws back your signing bonus). Brands have chemistry: prestige clashes sour the portfolio, sector synergies sweeten it.

## 11. MEDIA & SOCIAL

Your **media profile** (0–100) decays toward your tier's baseline — fame must be fed. Six named outlets hold stances toward you; post-race **interviews** with tone choices (humble, fiery, deflect…) move profile, outlets, sponsors, even the board — and spicy answers risk misquotes. Weekly **PR actions** (press releases, garage days, sponsor shoots that tick promise clauses, exclusives that make other outlets jealous). Three **social platforms** grow followers through results and a weekly content plan; viral moments spike everything. Exposure feeds sponsor interest, exhibition fees, and board patience — a beloved team is hard to sack.

## 12. RACE WEEKENDS — WHERE IT ALL LANDS

The week before: the **engineer's brief** (setup direction from track knowledge, weather, car state), the **stakes card** (mandate, sponsor clauses, title picture, your nemesis), and an **approach choice** — conservative, balanced, attack — with real consequences. Then the **Package Outlook**: every career factor — R&D, wear, track knowledge, crew, storylines, calibration — compiles into one honest readout of how strong the grid will be against you, written directly into the **exported AI XML** that AMS2 actually runs. Grids fill to each **venue's real capacity** — Le Mans means 46–48 cars. Drive the race. Telemetry commits the truth. The **debrief** reads your result against expectation, settles money, clauses, standings, and occasionally the **stewards** come calling. The field also **calibrates** to you over time — dominate three weekends and rival garages find pace; drown and the midfield softens — slow, capped, and always announced.

## 13. THE BROADCAST

Hit **GO LIVE** before a session and the companion commentator app receives your career: standings, storylines, your nemesis, your sponsor's podium clause. A two-voice booth (scaling from club-radio single commentator to full world-feed production as you climb) calls your race with anti-repetition memory, real pacing, and your actual season as its script. The post-race headline feeds back into your media world.

## 14. A LIVING SPORT

All 142 championships run whether you're there or not — results, champions, records, retirements, rising stars. **THE PADDOCK WEEK** digest brings the sport to your inbox, and the tier above you always has a story worth aspiring to. Rival **team principals** remember everything; push one far enough and you have a **nemesis** who races you harder — angrier and faster in the actual sim.

## 15. STORYLINES

262 data-driven events — staff crises, mystery brake failures, board ambushes, sponsor demands, paddock fires, regulation loopholes — with rarity tiers, multi-beat chained storylines, and **45 crossfire dilemmas** that deliberately pit your systems against each other. No right answers. Consequences only when *you* choose.

## 16. MANUFACTURERS

Race a marque's machinery well and their motorsport division notices. Climb the ladder: **parts discounts → customer support packages → a works-supported entry** with factory cash, factory engineers, and factory expectations. Earn enough standing and you'll be invited on **factory visits**. Works deals breed exclusivity tension — race a rival's car and expect a phone call.

## 17. LEGACY & ENDINGS

Twenty-five trophies into the **cabinet**, records into the **book**, every season bound into a **yearbook**, your team climbing a **seven-tier prestige ladder** from *Garage Outfit* to *Motorsport Dynasty*. Careers truly end: **collapse** in administration, **selling the team** at a price your empire earned, or **retiring** on your own terms — each scored out of 100 and enshrined in a **Hall of Fame** that outlives the save.

## 18. THE CABINET (PRESENTATION)

Every human has a deterministic generated portrait. The game has a full soundscape — UI, ambience, music (openly licensed, credited). Four career save slots behind a proper main menu (saves are atomic — a crash can't corrupt them), settings for audio/display/integration, and a UI rebuilt screen-by-screen to one standard: Home as a **command center**, dedicated screens for media, manufacturers, legacy, and staff on a bottom nav, a top bar that deep-links everywhere, and a layer of motion — transitions, number roll-ups, celebrations — with a **reduced-motion toggle** if you'd rather not.

## 19. YOUR FIRST HOUR

A **nine-step guided arc** walks your opening weeks — paced nudges at the right moments, a dedicated brief before your first race weekend, and per-screen walkthroughs the first time you open anything. All of it is optional and replayable, with a **hints toggle** when you've outgrown it.
